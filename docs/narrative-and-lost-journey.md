# Narrative canon: the lost journey

Status: **approved narrative direction, not a claim of implemented gameplay**. Source: owner story discussions, 2026-10-20/21. Related documentation issue #457. See [experience direction](experience-direction.md), [Archive and discovery](archive-recovery-and-navigation.md), [Absenat](absenat-and-hall.md), and [roadmap](experience-implementation-map.md).

## Premise and player knowledge

Salimon is a grounded cinematic science-fiction survival exploration sandbox with a vast, largely lonely universe. Each new run begins **outside a landed Scout on an unknown alien planet**, in the permanently chosen fictional starting system at least 30 light-years from Earth (future universe work #412/#426). The traveler has lost autobiographical memory of the journey, but knows they are a traveler, remembers Earth as **home**, and retains practical familiarity with operating their spaceship. Their personal identity/history, the landing, and what occurred after departing the Solar System are unknown. Do not force an explanation or imply practical amnesia.

**Earth is remembered but not charted**: the character does not know the current position or route to the Solar System. The game catalog's authoritative celestial coordinates must not implicitly become player discovery. The traveler has two viable independent long-term motivations: chart a route home to Earth, or follow clues to Absenat. They may also freely survive, travel and investigate unrelated locations. Neither storyline is a forced linear quest.

The Core remains lore canon: an unknown entity delivered a Core to Earth every 18 hours with the message **“Reach the Absenat, where everything started.”** Humanity used the extraordinary energy-storage technology; the Core, its sale/commercialization and the entity remain important to the larger mystery. Do not change origin/explanation without a later explicit decision. Absenat remains the final destination and gate ending.

## Last intact voyage evidence

The physical Ship Archive holds ordinary logs and messages before the data break:
1. Departure from Earth orbit.
2. Passing/transiting Mars.
3. Ordinary ship maintenance, provisioning and unrelated/personal communications. Mundane records make the lost life believable; do not replace them with only exposition.
4. Leaving Solar System territory, the last intact outbound boundary record.

These records establish a historical departure, **not** a present-day route back, nor necessarily the traveler's ultimate intentions. Avoid assigning unapproved dates, addresses, named family members or specific long-distance trajectory.

## Historical chronology versus what can be proven

After Solar System exit, the proposed underlying past is:
1. A distress transmission was received with a location; the traveler diverted to investigate.
2. At the destination they found evidence/clues suggesting a wormhole might lead somewhere near Absenat.
3. While approaching or moving toward that possible wormhole, a hostile/warning communication arrived from a different distant origin claiming it was a **trap** and advising against passage.
4. The traveler distrusted the warning and continued the approach.
5. The later traveler wakes on the unknown planet.

**Critical retained ambiguity:** Whether the traveler actually **entered** the wormhole is itself an unresolved mystery. Earlier descriptions of passage are not binding fact. Do not encode “traversed wormhole” as certain history, log truth, gameplay tutorial text, achievement, or foreshadowing. Equally undecided are the reason for waking on the planet, whether the ship landed itself, who may have helped, what caused the memory loss, and what happened during the missing interval.

Separate:
- **Observed evidence:** recorded received packets, verified navigational/timestamp records, physical traces.
- **Message claims:** distress, promised wormhole shortcut, “trap” warning; neither is automatically true.
- **Traveler inferences:** possible route near Absenat, motives of sender, whether they entered the anomaly.
- **Unknown:** actual senders, intentions, origin and physics of wormhole, event sequence after approach.

## Opening recovery and investigation

The player encounters a physical Archive computer in the familiar Scout, then a power-hungry recovery need that motivates resource exploration. The approved first damaged-message investigations, in player-facing **recovery order**:
- **Recovered message 1:** location tied to the distress/accident site, giving a planetary investigation lead and possible evidence of the wormhole.
- **Recovered message 2:** a threatening or warning transmission from a separate distant location, providing a second place to explore. It asserts “trap” but its truth remains unverified.
- Further corrupted messages and broader clue chronology **are deliberately unspecified**; do not invent additional canonical revelations, mandatory steps or an automatic Absenat coordinate.

The chronology of the original traveler and the order in which the current player recovers fragments are different concepts. Site discoveries should deepen ambiguity and offer evidence, not require combat, NPC encounters or incessant quest markers. A possible wormhole pointing near Absenat is **not** proof of its destination.

## Narrative delivery and anti-spoiler rules

Favor inspectable logs, physical traces, instrument context, quiet environmental storytelling, and credible routine details. Avoid lore dumps, forced dialogue, constant objectives and exposition that knows more than the traveler. A recovered message should help the player investigate, not automatically fill every missing fact. No Hall of Absenat, gate interior or inscription mechanic is revealed in pre-completion UI, menus, tutorials, achievements, hidden disabled entries or incidental hints.

## Existing-document reconciliation

The older [project vision](project-vision.md) Earth spawn and early Mars-first-quest assumptions are historical and superseded **for the new future opening**, not evidence that the current prototype already uses the alien start. The [approved universe expansion](universe-expansion-approved-design.md) provides the new homeworld and real-scale environment; the story adds memory loss and uncharted origins without changing astronomical truth. Neither the Absenat gate nor the wormhole is ordinary fast travel available on the ship. Any technology that traverses such a passage requires its own future, explicit physics/gameplay contract.
